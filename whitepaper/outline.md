# Whitepaper Outline

## Working title

AI Half-Life: Measuring Value Decay in Deployed Artificial Intelligence Systems

## Subtitle

A practical framework for evaluating whether AI systems continue to create value after launch.

## Executive summary

Healthcare and enterprise AI have moved beyond experimentation. Organizations are deploying predictive models, ambient documentation tools, recommendation systems, robotic optimization systems, fraud detection tools, and generative AI assistants into live workflows.

The field has developed substantial guidance for responsible AI, governance, safety, fairness, bias, model performance, and drift. Yet a critical measurement problem remains underdeveloped: how long does an AI system continue creating realized value after deployment?

This whitepaper proposes AI Half-Life as a practical framework for measuring the time it takes for a deployed AI solution to lose 50 percent of its realized value.

## Section 1: The deployment era

- AI has moved from pilot to production.
- Healthcare and industry are reporting measurable AI outcomes.
- Deployment alone does not prove sustained value.

## Section 2: What we measure today

- Technical performance
- Drift
- Bias and fairness
- Governance
- Safety
- Deployment adoption

## Section 3: What we do not measure well

- Long-term adoption decay
- Workflow decay
- Trust decay
- Financial value erosion
- Governance fatigue
- Retirement criteria
- Time-to-value-failure

## Section 4: Defining AI Half-Life

AI Half-Life is the amount of time required for a deployed AI system to lose 50 percent of its realized value.

This section distinguishes:

- Statistical half-life
- Operational half-life
- Financial/value half-life

## Section 7: Early findings

Expected findings to test:

- Public reporting emphasizes launch and short-term outcomes.
- Technical metrics are better reported than long-term value metrics.
- Adoption and workflow measures appear repeatedly but inconsistently.
- Follow-up duration is often short.
- Retirement and recalibration are rarely reported openly.

## Section 8: Mathematical approach

Discuss:

- Decay curves
- Survival analysis
- Reliability engineering
- Statistical process control
- Bayesian updating
- Value-of-information analysis

## Section 9: Governance implications

AI governance should include value persistence reviews.

Potential governance questions:

- Is the system still being used?
- Is it still integrated into the workflow?
- Is it still financially justified?
- Has the model drifted?
- Has the workflow changed?
- Has sponsorship changed?
- Should we recalibrate, redesign, restrict, or retire?

## Section 10: Future research

- Expand to 100 verified deployments
- Build value persistence curves
- Develop open-source calculator
- Test survival-analysis approach
- Build contribution process for public case submissions

## Conclusion

AI Half-Life is not proposed as a finished universal law. It is proposed as a practical measurement construct for a problem organizations already face: deployed AI systems can lose value long before anyone formally turns them off.
