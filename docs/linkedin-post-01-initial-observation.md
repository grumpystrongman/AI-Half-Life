# LinkedIn Post 01: Initial Observation

Over the last few weeks I've been reviewing healthcare AI governance frameworks, implementation studies, model monitoring literature, and industry adoption research.

Something keeps bothering me.

Healthcare has become increasingly sophisticated at measuring AI performance.

We measure accuracy, precision, recall, bias, fairness, and drift.

We're also getting better at measuring governance, risk, and adoption.

But I keep coming back to a different question:

How do we know when an AI solution stops creating value?

Not when accuracy drops.

Not when drift appears.

When the actual value disappears.

I've seen analytics products and AI solutions that were technically healthy but operationally dead.

The model still worked.

The workflow changed.

Users stopped trusting it.

Leadership priorities shifted.

Adoption faded.

The projected ROI never materialized, or slowly eroded over time.

As I review the literature, I'm finding extensive guidance around governance, safety, monitoring, and responsible AI.

What I'm not finding is a consistent framework for measuring the decline of realized value after deployment.

I'm beginning to wonder whether we need a concept similar to half-life in physics:

The amount of time required for an AI solution to lose 50 percent of its realized value.

Still researching.

Still testing the idea.

Curious:

How does your organization determine whether an AI solution is still delivering meaningful value two or three years after deployment?
